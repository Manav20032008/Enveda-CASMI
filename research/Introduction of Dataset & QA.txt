Q.How to change the m/z ?
->  1.change mass(via adduct) :
    -Protonation/Deprotonation: Adding a proton (H+) increases the mass by ≈1.007 u.      
    For a singly charged ion [M+H]+, the m/z is M+1.007. 
    
    -Metal Adducts: Attaching metal ions like Sodium (Na+,≈22.99 u) or Potassium (K+,≈39.10 u) more increases the m/z value compared to the protonated form.
    
    2.change charge(Electrospray Ionization) 
    
    3.fragmentaion
    Breaking the molecule into smaller pieces changes the mass m of the resulting ions.

    Fragment Ions: If a molecular ion with mass M fragments, the resulting smaller ions will have lower m/z values.
    Neutral Loss: The loss of neutral fragments (like water or methyl groups) from the molecular ion creates peaks at lower m/z values corresponding to the remaining charged fragment.

2. The Fragmentation (The Core Match)
{ms2_mzs & ms2_normalized_intensities}:
The m/z values of the fragments paired with their relative intensities form a unique spectral fingerprint. Find similar entry from the databaase.

3. The Experimental values (Ensuring an Accurate Match)
{collision_energy_ev & instrument_type & base_peak_intensity}:
Molecules fragment differently depending on how much energy is slammed into them, and different mass spectrometers handle fragmentation slightly differently.Base peak will helps with quality control, ensuring the signal to noise ratio is high enough to trust the fragment peaks.
